# Speaking track

Technical depth that cannot be said clearly in an interview is invisible. This track trains the speaking side the same way the labs train the hands-on side: with a fixed method, short repetitions, and evidence of progress.

## The problem it solves

Engineers with deep experience often lose interviews in three predictable ways:

1. **Starting in the middle.** The answer begins with tool names before the listener knows the point.
2. **Stopping too early.** The answer ends at "and it worked", without the trade-off, the failure mode, or why it mattered to the customer.
3. **Freezing under a hard question.** Silence while thinking reads as not knowing, even when the knowledge is there.

Each has a specific fix below.

## How it connects to the labs

Every lab ends with a `TALK.md`. Doing the lab gives you the substance; saying the `TALK.md` out loud, with a timer and a recording, makes it yours. Do both. A lab you built but never explained aloud will still come out as a ramble in the interview.

## Three rules to remember on the day

1. **Headline first.** Your first sentence is the answer. Detail comes after.
2. **Pause, don't fill.** A two-second pause sounds like thinking. "Um, so, basically" sounds like guessing.
3. **Finish with why.** End on the customer impact or the trade-off, then stop talking.
